Zaynab Azeem

University of Toronto. Systems and Behavioural Neuroscience.

I am a Neuroscience student at the University of Toronto, specializing in Systems and Behavioural Neuroscience.

I study perception and representation learning, focusing on how the brain encodes and interprets visual information. My bachelor’s thesis in the Neurocomputational Vision Lab with Professor Adrian Nestor uses neurally guided GAN synthesis to reconstruct human faces. By aligning EEG-derived neural activity with the latent space of models such as StyleGAN2, I investigate how facial information is structured and represented in the brain.

I also work in the Visual Recognition Lab with Professor Jonathan Cant, where I study the neural and behavioural mechanisms of object and scene perception. I am currently co-authoring a review on how context shapes the brain’s integration of global and local visual information.

Previously, at the Keenan Research Centre with Dr. Yeni Yücel, I led the first in vivo quantification of cerebrospinal fluid drainage in a NASA microgravity model, work that received multiple poster awards and a travel grant to the Canadian Space Health Research Symposium.

I care deeply about bridging computational methods with neuroscience to advance our understanding of how the brain creates meaning from the world.

Research Interests

I am currently thinking a lot about:

  • Perception and Neural Representation: How the brain encodes and organizes visual information—from faces to complex scenes—and how these representations adapt to context.
  • Computational Modeling: Using generative models and neural data (EEG, fMRI) to link brain activity with high-dimensional representational spaces.
  • Human and Artificial Intelligence: Exploring how principles of human perception can inspire more interpretable and biologically grounded AI systems.
