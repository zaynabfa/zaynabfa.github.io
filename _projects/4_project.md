## Evaluating EEG-EMG Connectivity Measures During Static Motor Tasks

This ongoing research project (2025) at the Motion and Adaptation Science Laboratory, University Health Network, focuses on developing signal processing pipelines to analyze EEG-EMG connectivity during motor tasks. The work has applications for understanding motor control and informing rehabilitation approaches in movement disorders.

### Project Overview

The project aims to develop and validate novel methods for analyzing neural-muscular communication through EEG-EMG connectivity measures. This research contributes to our understanding of motor control mechanisms and has potential applications in rehabilitation medicine.

### Technical Approach

The research employs advanced signal processing techniques:

- **MATLAB Development**: Creating comprehensive signal processing pipelines for EEG-EMG analysis
- **RMS Normalization**: Computing RMS-normalized EMG (%MVC) for standardized muscle activity measures
- **Corticomuscular Coherence (CMC)**: Modeling neural-muscular synchronization patterns
- **Phase-Amplitude Coupling (PAC)**: Analyzing cross-frequency coupling between neural oscillations

### Experimental Protocol

The study examines connectivity during three distinct conditions:

1. **Rest State**: Baseline neural-muscular activity
2. **Plantarflexion**: Controlled ankle muscle activation
3. **Dorsiflexion**: Controlled ankle muscle activation in opposite direction

### Research Applications

This work has significant implications for:

- **Motor Control Understanding**: Advancing knowledge of how the brain controls muscle activity
- **Rehabilitation Medicine**: Informing therapeutic approaches for movement disorders
- **Neural Interfaces**: Contributing to brain-computer interface development
- **Clinical Assessment**: Developing biomarkers for motor function evaluation

### Methodological Innovation

The project focuses on:

- **Signal Quality**: Ensuring high-fidelity EEG-EMG recordings
- **Connectivity Metrics**: Developing robust measures of neural-muscular communication
- **Statistical Analysis**: Applying appropriate statistical methods for connectivity assessment
- **Validation**: Comparing different connectivity measures for reliability and validity

### Current Status

The project is ongoing with active development of:

- Signal preprocessing algorithms
- Connectivity analysis methods
- Statistical validation procedures
- Clinical application protocols

This research represents an important contribution to the field of motor neuroscience and has the potential to improve our understanding of neural-muscular communication in both healthy and pathological conditions.
