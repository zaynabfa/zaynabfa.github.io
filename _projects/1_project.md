## Investigating the Geometry and Topology of Face Representations

This ongoing research project explores how different race categories are organized in representational manifolds within the human brain. Using advanced computational approaches, I investigate the geometric structure of face space and its implications for multi-race face perception.

### Key Research Questions

- How are different race categories organized in high-dimensional neural representational spaces?
- What geometric properties characterize the manifold structure of face representations?
- How do these structures relate to behavioral phenomena like the other-race effect?

### Technical Approach

- **Riemannian Metrics**: Applying differential geometry to analyze the curvature and structure of face representational spaces
- **Similarity-Structure Analysis**: Evaluating clustering, separability, and continuity in high-dimensional face spaces
- **Cross-Group Comparisons**: Developing computational pipelines to compare manifold structures across participant groups and datasets
- **Behavioral Correlates**: Linking geometric properties to perceptual and cognitive phenomena

### Impact and Applications

This research has significant implications for understanding:

- The neural basis of face recognition and categorization
- Individual differences in face perception abilities
- The stability of neural representations across different contexts
- Applications in computer vision and artificial intelligence

### Current Status

The project is ongoing with preliminary results showing distinct geometric properties in face space organization across different race categories. Findings are being prepared for presentation at the Canadian Association for Neuroscience (CAN) 2025 Annual Meeting.
